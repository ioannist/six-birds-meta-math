## Candidate A: a SAT measure observed by the same Gaussian

Take an actual CNF formula `φ` with `n` variables and the Boolean verifier now defined in `six-birds-hiddenness/lean/SixBirdsHiddenness/PvNP/CNFSemantics.lean`. Choose a concrete, efficiently describable map `z_n` from assignments to spectral coordinates. Form the finite positive measure

`μ_φ = Σ_{a : {0,1}^n} [evalFormula a φ] · δ_{z_n(a)}`.

This measure is defined from a cheap *candidate verifier* but can have exponentially many terms. For a strictly positive Gaussian weight `K_{a,b}(z)`, its scalar observation

`T_{a,b}(φ) = ∫ K_{a,b}(z) dμ_φ(z) = Σ_{a satisfies φ} K_{a,b}(z_n(a))`

is positive exactly when `φ` is satisfiable. The implication uses positivity of each Gaussian term and finiteness; it does **not** give a polynomial-time evaluator for the sum. An implementation of `μ_φ` by explicit enumeration is exponential in `n`.

The existing pointwise complex Gaussian/Navier identity should transport across the finite sum. The stronger actual-state target is to sum `complex_nonlinear_frame_return_at_point` over the assignment coordinates and retain every Duhamel term. The zero-window result uses the same observation family on its actual zeta-zero measure. That would give a proved common operator on three native, explicitly constructed carriers, with domain-specific returns. The claim must state the precise operator relation and must not merge their source laws.

The assignment-coordinate choice matters. If the coordinates use exponentially large integers, some Gaussian weights become extremely small and an approximate positivity test may need prohibitive precision. If they are packed into a bounded interval, separation may be exponentially small. An exact positive readout is mathematical information; a lawful uniform SAT decider needs cost and error control. This cost is the candidate's main residual, not a footnote.

## Candidate B: Gaussian sign projection and Boolean noise

An alternative route is to derive Boolean assignment bits as signs of Gaussian coordinates, and compare a correlated Gaussian pair with a Boolean noise coupling. The one-time sign correlation of a bivariate centered normal with correlation `r` is the nontrivial function `(2/π) arcsin(r)`. Under an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck correlation `r=e^{-t}`, the induced Boolean correlation is `(2/π) arcsin(e^{-t})`, which generally fails the multiplicative semigroup law. Thus a one-time observation bridge is plausible, while a direct semigroup intertwining through signs is an obstruction to test, not an identity to assume.

To use this route, prove the Gaussian orthant identity from the actual measure model, formulate the exact Boolean operator on a finite cube, and return its residual to concrete SAT predicates with all computational costs exposed. The current code has neither of those theorems. If this construction cannot connect to the retained RH zero-window and actual Navier source return at a substantive level, it is a diagnostic, not the triad result.
